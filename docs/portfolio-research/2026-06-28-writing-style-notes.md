# Writing Style Notes For Future Portfolio Essays

Date: 2026-06-28
Purpose: capture how the reference sites write so future posts on your portfolio feel personal, thoughtful, and readable.

## What Good Personal-Technical Writing Is Doing

Across the references, good writing is usually doing four things at once:
- explaining something real
- revealing the writer's taste
- exposing uncertainty or evolution
- making the reader feel like they are inside the process

The writing is rarely just informational. It is interpretive.

## Reference Voice Patterns

## Lee Robinson
Source pages:
- https://leerob.com/
- https://leerob.com/writing
- https://leerob.com/agents

### Voice characteristics
- conversational, but disciplined
- technically clear
- persuasive without sounding salesy
- anchored in specifics: cost, time, tools, tradeoffs, constraints
- opinionated, but the opinion arrives through examples

### Structural habits
- starts from a real moment or problem
- introduces a tension
- breaks ideas into clean sections with strong headings
- moves from anecdote to general principle
- ends with a broader implication

### Useful imitation pattern
Use when writing technical project essays:
1. open with a real moment: a bug, a design tension, a failed attempt, a surprising result
2. state the bigger question
3. explain the system in plain language
4. show one or two concrete details
5. explain what changed in your understanding
6. close with the broader lesson

## Aidan Smith
Source pages:
- https://aidanjs.com/about
- https://aidanjs.com/blog

### Voice characteristics
- direct and personal
- slightly literary, slightly chaotic in a good way
- unafraid of side details, quirks, intensity, humor
- feels written by a person with an interior life, not an optimized founder bio

### Useful lessons
- Mix serious work with personal texture.
- Let lines breathe.
- You do not need every paragraph to sound efficient.
- A little self-awareness makes technical people feel more human.

### Good pattern to borrow
After a dense section, insert one short human sentence that changes the temperature.

Examples of the pattern, not text to copy:
- "This part looked elegant on paper. In practice it fought back."
- "I thought the hard part would be modeling. It turned out to be memory."
- "There is a version of this story where the system works on day one. This is not that version."

## Arjun Virk
Source cues from repo content and writing organization.

### Voice characteristics
- serious but not stiff
- research-minded
- concise and intentional
- gives concepts room without over-explaining them

### Useful lessons
- Write like someone building a body of thought, not random posts.
- Categories can help establish intellectual territory.
- Reflections do not need to sound polished to sound valuable.

### Good pattern to borrow
Use category-level framing like:
- field notes
- project reflections
- systems essays
- experiments
- research fragments

This makes the writing section feel authored, not dumped.

## Frank
Source pages:
- https://www.okfrank.co/

### Voice characteristics
- polished, selective, understated confidence
- sentences often carry a clear sense of movement: from place to place, product to product, idea to idea
- uses links as proof rather than explanatory clutter

### Useful lessons
- Summary writing can still feel alive.
- You can write a career arc as a sequence of ideas, not just roles.
- Fewer claims, better phrased, feels stronger than exhaustive explanation.

## What To Avoid In Your Own Writing

## 1. Resume voice
Avoid writing that sounds like:
- built X using Y and Z
- achieved A by leveraging B
- developed an innovative solution

That voice kills intimacy and makes the writing sound interchangeable.

## 2. Essay voice that is too formal
Avoid sounding like:
- a class report
- a medium post trying to look smart
- a research abstract pretending to be a story

## 3. Over-explaining every term
Your best readers will tolerate some inference. Clarity matters, but over-translation weakens authority.

## 4. Purely triumphant storytelling
The good references feel credible because they include:
- doubt
- constraints
- false starts
- tradeoffs
- changed opinions

## A Writing Formula That Fits Your Portfolio

For project essays, this shape seems strongest:

### 1. The pull
Open with the puzzle, tension, or observation.
- What kept bothering you?
- What was broken in the usual approach?
- Why was this more interesting than a normal software project?

### 2. The setting
Give just enough context.
- what system
- what domain
- why it matters

### 3. The hard part
Name the actual difficulty.
This is often where the best writing starts.
Examples for your work:
- time changes language
- systems forget context
- browser failures are noisy, not obvious
- educational mismatch is not just a document problem, but a structure problem

### 4. The build
Explain what you made in plain language.
- not every file
- not the full stack list
- just the core mechanism

### 5. The surprise
What did you learn that you did not expect?
This is where the essay becomes yours.

### 6. The afterthought
End with what the project changed in your thinking.
Not just "future work," but "what I now believe."

## Sentence-Level Notes

### Good sentence patterns
- short, declarative sentence after a dense paragraph
- contrast sentence: "I expected X. The real issue was Y."
- escalation sentence: "The problem was not extraction. It was memory."
- perspective shift: "At that point, this stopped feeling like a modeling problem."

### Good rhythm patterns
- mix long explanatory paragraphs with short impact lines
- use section headers that make a claim, not just a label
- let one line occasionally stand alone if it earns the emphasis

### Useful title patterns
Better than generic project titles:
- `What Browser Failures Actually Look Like`
- `Why Longitudinal Systems Need Memory, Not Just Extraction`
- `Building an Agent That Watches Before It Acts`
- `What I Learned Trying to Compare Curricula Automatically`
- `When a Timeline Becomes the Product`

These are examples of shape, not final titles.

## Tone Sliders For Your Future Posts

You can think of each post on three sliders:

### 1. Technical depth
- light: broad explanation for general audience
- medium: system concepts + selected implementation detail
- deep: detailed architecture / modeling / evaluation writeup

### 2. Personal presence
- low: mostly explanatory
- medium: includes decisions, surprises, doubts
- high: reflective, diary-like, openly subjective

### 3. Artifact density
- low: mostly text
- medium: text + screenshots/diagram
- high: text + diagrams + logs + outputs + links

For your portfolio, the strongest default is probably:
- technical depth: medium
- personal presence: medium
- artifact density: medium to high
